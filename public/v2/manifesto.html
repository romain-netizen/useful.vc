Manifesto

Find the companies doing work that matters. Help them do more of it.

useful.vc is a public map of technical work worth doing—and a way to direct capable people towards it.

What is useful?

Staying alive. A body that works. A child who can develop. The ability to move, think, communicate, work and remain independent.

Food, water and energy that remain available. Land and living systems that continue to support life. Technologies that prevent serious harm, restore what has been lost or make an essential capability possible for the first time.

These are not fashionable causes. They are the foundations on which everything else depends.

Not everything that matters belongs on a company list

Love matters. Family, friendship, freedom and dignity matter. They may matter more than anything a company can produce.

But they cannot be reduced honestly to a company score. useful.vc is deliberately narrower. We look for products that perform consequential work in the world: extending life, preserving health and capacity, protecting living systems, or breaking a technical limit that prevents essential things from becoming possible, reliable or widely accessible.

Why useful.vc exists

Around every serious problem, an economy develops.

Some companies work on the problem itself. Others measure it, report it, certify it, finance it, insure it, trade it, produce presentations about it or help someone else promise that it may eventually improve.

The second group is often easier to fund. Its revenue arrives earlier. Its risks are lower. Its claims are harder to disprove. The person or living system carrying the problem may remain exactly where it was.

useful.vc exists to separate the companies performing consequential work from the companies operating around it.

Not perfectly. Publicly.

Important problems come first

We do not begin with sectors, trends or political priorities. We begin with the problem.

Does it threaten life? Does it damage health, development, independence or an important capacity? Does it weaken the ability of a living system to persist and regenerate? Does it expose a genuine technical limit in an essential physical capability?

Severity comes before popularity. Irreversible harm comes before reversible inconvenience. A serious outcome affecting a smaller population may matter more than a minor efficiency multiplied by a theoretical global market.

Not every improvement is important enough for this list. That is deliberate.

Technology changes what is possible

We believe in technology because some problems cannot be solved by better intentions, better communication or better allocation of existing tools. They require a new tool.

A more accurate diagnosis. A safer medical intervention. A machine that restores a lost capacity. A biological model that makes consequential research possible. A step-change in energy generation, storage, materials, food production or resource recovery.

Technology is not automatically useful. Novelty is not enough. A patent is not enough. Artificial intelligence is not enough. Physical hardware is not enough.

But when a product creates a material capability that did not exist before, removes a real bottleneck or makes an essential outcome substantially better or more accessible, that matters.

Directness is not usefulness

The product does not have to perform the final act. A clinician may decide. A scientist may develop the treatment. An operator may use the machine. A farmer may act on the result.

The question is not whether the company controls every person in the chain. The question is whether removing its product would make an important outcome materially less likely, worse, slower, less accessible or less durable.

Information can perform useful work. Detection, diagnosis, prediction, selection and guidance can matter enormously when they materially change a consequential decision. An upstream technology can matter when it removes a binding technical obstacle and its output is converted into real downstream work.

A report that someone may ignore is not enough. A product that materially changes what can be known or done may be.

We are not anti-climate. We are anti-proxy.

Carbon is a measurement. It is not a person, a living system or a universal moral currency.

A calculated quantity of avoided emissions does not make a company useful. Nor does a carbon credit, an offset, a disclosure, a target, a certificate or a policy-created market.

The underlying physical work is what matters.

A technology that creates a material new energy capability can qualify. A technology that restores a living system can qualify. A process that overcomes a serious resource constraint can qualify.

Ordinary optimisation, reporting, leasing, installation and incremental efficiency do not qualify merely because an emissions number can be attached to them. Politics may encourage useful work. It cannot manufacture usefulness.

Building is different from deploying

Installing an available product can be valuable. Financing it can be valuable. Distributing, leasing, operating or integrating it can be valuable.

But useful.vc is looking for the companies that change what the technology can do or how an important outcome can be produced.

Remove the company’s own technology. Could essentially the same result and advantage be obtained from existing products and interchangeable labour?

If so, the company is deploying an existing capability rather than building a new one.

We make that distinction because brilliant technical people are scarce. They should be able to find the places where their work can expand the boundary of what is possible.

Evidence before intention

Good intentions confer nothing.

Mission statements, impact reports, political approval, founder reputation and investor enthusiasm do not prove that a product is useful. Neither does revenue, adoption, regulatory clearance, publication or technical accuracy by itself.

The evidence must establish the material contribution made by the actual product in its intended use. The form of evidence depends on the product, but the standard is constant: make the contribution specific, compare it with what would otherwise happen, expose uncertainty and show the strongest contradictory evidence.

A list you can argue with

Every judgement is public. The product being judged is identified. The important outcome is stated. The alternative is named. Each rule is answered. Evidence and limitations are shown. Missing information is visible. Conflicts are disclosed. A named person signs the decision.

Our own investments receive no advantage.

A decision can be challenged with evidence. If the evidence changes the judgement, the entry changes. The previous decision remains in the history.

No silent edits. No protected companies. No authority without reasoning.

The objective is not to create a list that cannot be challenged. It is to create one that can only be challenged in the open.

A collaborative project, not a political movement

useful.vc is open to people of every country, background and profession.

It is not a political coalition, a culture-war forum, an ESG scorecard or a place to perform moral alignment.

We do not ask contributors to agree on parties, identities, institutions or fashionable causes. We ask them to agree on a method:

  • important problems before fashionable ones;
  • real outcomes before reported ones;
  • evidence before affiliation;
  • invention before deployment;
  • transparency before authority;
  • action before signalling.

Methodological alignment is required. Ideological alignment is irrelevant.

Criticise claims, evidence and reasoning. Do not attack people. Disclose conflicts. State uncertainty. Change your mind when the facts change.

Who should join

This community is not for everyone.

It is for engineers, scientists, clinicians, operators, founders, researchers, investors and students who believe that technical progress remains one of the strongest ways to improve human life.

It is for people who can distinguish a difficult, consequential problem from a fashionable one. People who would rather test a mechanism than repeat a slogan. People who can recognise useful work even when it is unpopular, and reject an attractive narrative when the underlying work is weak.

People who do not merely want to discuss important companies, but want to help them.

Put your mind to work

Submit a company. Improve an incomplete profile. Add stronger evidence. Identify a product that has been judged too broadly. Challenge a counterfactual. Correct a factual error. Review a technical claim. Introduce a customer. Introduce an exceptional candidate.

Join one of the companies. Or build the one that is missing.

useful.vc is not a badge and not a community built around shared self-image.

It is a map of work worth doing—and a way to direct capable people towards it.
