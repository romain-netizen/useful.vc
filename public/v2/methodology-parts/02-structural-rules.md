## 5. The five structural rules

Any one rule firing makes the product-use unit Excluded. There is no averaging, weighting, compensation or discretionary override.

A structural pass does not claim that the contribution has already been proved. It means that the existing product, intended use, pathway, technical mechanism and economic model can satisfy the rule without becoming a different product or business. Missing verification is handled by readiness.

### R1 — The outcome is important enough

The product must pass either the essential-outcome route or the frontier-capability route in §3.

The claimed benefit must be primary or inseparable, material at realistic scale and assessed against the importance order.

A product fails R1 where its claimed value is principally comfort, convenience, ordinary output, administrative improvement, operational efficiency, utilisation, incremental resource reduction or another lower-order benefit that does not cross the essential-outcome or frontier-capability threshold.

A product also fails R1 where the serious outcome appears only through a remote chain of optional assumptions or hypothetical scale.

**Direct physical action does not cure an R1 failure.**

### R2 — The product makes a material additional contribution

Remove the product from the ordinary pathway while holding the other actors, incentives and available alternatives constant.

The product passes only where its absence is claimed to materially worsen at least one of the following:

- the probability of the qualifying outcome;
- the quality or magnitude of the outcome;
- the time at which it occurs;
- its accessibility, continuity or durability;
- the reliability of a necessary consequential decision or technical step;
- the feasible performance of an essential physical capability.

For a structural pass, this removal effect must be specific, technically plausible, externally testable and achievable by the product as it exists without requiring a different product or business model. Independent measurement of the effect is a readiness question. Where strong evidence already shows that the effect is below the threshold, R2 fails.

The comparison is made against both the relevant outcome counterfactual and, where applicable, the technical frontier reference.

The product does not have to perform or control the final intervention. Another party may retain judgement and responsibility. What matters is whether the product performs material work that changes the pathway.

#### Informational and decision contributions

An informational product can pass where:

1. its intended use identifies a defined consequential decision, selection, detection, prioritisation or response;
2. its output is specific enough to change that step rather than merely describe the problem;
3. the product-specific change in that step is externally testable;
4. a specific, scientifically defensible and externally testable causal bridge connects the changed step to the qualifying outcome;
5. the required magnitude of change is stated before the verdict.

The fact that a professional can reject or override an output does not break the pathway. The test is the product’s measured counterfactual contribution under normal use, not control over the professional.

An output fails where it supplies only a generic possibility, report, score, alert or option with no defined consequential use, no testable conversion into action and no material removal effect.

#### Enabling and upstream contributions

An enabling product can pass where:

1. it changes a necessary technical step in a defined pathway to a qualifying outcome;
2. it materially changes a binding bottleneck against the best relevant alternative;
3. its output is specific to that pathway rather than a generic future possibility;
4. external use, verification or conversion into consequential downstream work is observable or governed by a precise verification route;
5. the causal bridge and materiality threshold are stated.

Causal distance raises the evidence burden. It does not create automatic exclusion.

The more contingent downstream decisions remain, the larger and better-evidenced the company’s contribution must be. A platform whose useful conversion depends on an unspecified future product, customer choice or deployment not yet defined fails R2.

#### Access and resource contributions

Lower cost, lower resource use or faster delivery can constitute a material contribution only under the value conditions in §4.4. Availability of savings to a payer is not enough.

#### Evidence of no contribution

Strong evidence that the current product does not meet the stated materiality threshold causes R2 to fail. Missing evidence alone does not.

### R3 — The claimed value is real and intrinsic

The product’s immediate output may be informational, but the claimed qualifying value must terminate in a defined real endpoint through a specific, scientifically defensible and externally testable bridge into:

- a biological or functional outcome;
- a change in the persistence, regeneration or life-supporting capacity of a living system;
- a durable physical change needed by living beings or systems;
- a material frontier advance in an essential physical capability.

The bridge need not yet be independently verified for a structural pass. Missing verification is a readiness gap. R3 fails where the product’s intended use ends in representation, transaction or administration, or where reaching a real endpoint depends on an unspecified future use rather than the defined product-use unit.

A change only in money, ownership, financing, risk allocation, legal entitlement, certification, accounting, reporting, compliance, exchange, contractual status or recorded impact is not a qualifying outcome. R3 fails.

A derived quantity is evidence, not an affected entity and not an outcome by itself. A quantity of avoided or removed emissions therefore cannot carry a verdict on its own. The engine must identify the additional physical or biological change, the entity that benefits, the relevant counterfactual, durability, transferred harms and realistic scale.

Where a claimed advantage disappears when a regulation, mandate, subsidy, disclosure obligation, accounting convention or regulation-made market is withdrawn, and no qualifying outcome or technical capability remains, R3 fails.

A rule may encourage or pay for real work without affecting the verdict. The test is whether the underlying contribution remains real when the rule-made representation or payment is removed.

### R4 — The company builds the technical leverage

The material contribution must depend on a repeatable company-specific product, production process or operated technical system.

The company does not have to invent every component, own a patent, manufacture every part or remove people from the pathway. It must materially change what the combined technology can do, how reliably it can do it, or how the qualifying outcome is produced.

Apply both tests.

#### Technical removal test

Remove the company’s own technical product and process.

If materially the same contribution and claimed advantage can be obtained at comparable quality, reliability, speed, scale and resource use from available products, ordinary integration and interchangeable labour, the company is deploying rather than building. R4 fails.

#### Scaling test

If delivery of the claimed contribution scales principally by adding interchangeable skilled human hours rather than by reproducing, operating or using the company’s technical capability, human work is the principal production mechanism. R4 fails.

Human work may install, supervise, maintain, interpret or use the product. It may perform the final intervention. This does not cause failure where the technical capability remains necessary to the material difference and materially changes what the person can reliably achieve.

Financing, leasing, ownership, distribution, installation or operation of existing technology does not become a company-built technical capability merely because it is organised well.

A proprietary process may pass by materially improving how an established qualifying outcome is produced. A cost or resource claim must also satisfy the value and evidence requirements.

### R5 — The economics align with the claimed outcome

Assess the economics at the level of one affected unit or one unit of the qualifying problem. Hold the number of affected units, their baseline condition and the contracted scope constant.

The company’s expected revenue, margin or lifetime economic value must not increase because the claimed outcome:

- is worse;
- arrives later;
- fails sooner;
- recurs avoidably;
- remains unresolved beyond the period necessary to preserve the benefit;
- is transferred to another affected entity.

All material economics linked to the same product, problem and affected unit are included, even when booked under another contract or payer.

The company may earn more by helping more affected units. The existence or prevalence of the problem is not by itself a failure.

Recurring payment can align where continued use continues to preserve, substitute, restore or sustain the benefit. It fails where the model improves through avoidable deterioration or repeated correction of the product’s own preventable failure.

The payer and contract form are irrelevant. An outcome-linked contract may support the analysis but never proves alignment.
